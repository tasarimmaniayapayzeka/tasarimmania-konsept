From Photograph to Product Video: How Does the Process Work?

The process starts with a high-resolution product photograph being uploaded.

The AI model first extracts a depth and material map of the object; the gloss of the surface, its texture and how it behaves in shadow are all established at this stage. Next the camera move is defined: one of a set of movement types — an orbit, a dolly in, or a macro sweep — is chosen and applied to the scene. As the diffusion-based video model brings that movement scenario to life frame by frame, it cross-checks light and shadow consistency against the reference photograph. A revision doesn't call for a reshoot; the prompt and the reference layer are updated and the scene is rendered again.

02

Brand Consistency: How Are Colour, Tone and Logo Held?

Brand consistency is secured by a style guide defined before production begins.

The colour palette is given to the model as a reference in hex codes, while tone and composition are pinned down through sample frames. The logo and the brand wording are not generated by the AI; they are laid over the scene as a separate layer at the final stage, which removes any risk of distortion or of the text being unreadable. Every scene is compared side by side with the reference frame and signed off before it goes live. So that the colour temperature and the colour balance stay consistent between different product videos, a shared colour grade profile is applied at the finishing stage. The generated scene is an advert too; Türkiye's commercial advertising rules cover misleading depiction.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions: AI-assisted video production

How is an AI-generated video told apart from real footage — will the viewer notice?

Scenes produced with the right references and light matching usually read as filmed in short advertising formats. The difference can be more visible in long, complex human movement; that's why product, location and abstract narrative scenes are the best fit for this line.

Is it really possible to build a product video from a photograph, and how does it work?

Yes — a single product photograph can be source enough. The model extracts depth and surface information from the image, applies the defined camera move to the scene on that basis, and produces a consistent video by comparing the result against the reference photograph.

How does the brand look stay the same across videos made on different projects and dates?

The style guide, the colour codes and the approved reference frames are kept in the project file. When a new video is requested the same references are used again, so even a scene produced later comes out with the same tone and palette as the first video.

In which cases does AI-assisted production not replace conventional filming?

For real human performance, live event moments or scenes calling for complex emotional expression, conventional filming still gives the more reliable result. For needs like these a hybrid approach is the recommendation: live filming and AI-assisted scenes used together on the same project.

Is the revision process for an AI-generated video different from conventional filming?

Different, and quicker. Where a revision in conventional filming means replanning and reshooting, on the AI line the scene parameters or the prompt are updated and a new render is taken from the same project.

Does AI video production carry any copyright or usage-rights risk?

It varies with the model and the type of licence used; that's why models with clear commercial usage rights are the ones chosen at the start of a project. Because the brand logo and wording are already added on a separate layer, no third-party content risk arises on those elements.

The detail

If you'd like to look before deciding

  1. 01

    Which Products and Sectors It Suits

    E-commerce product presentation, detail-led categories such as cosmetics and electronics, and location or concept presentation are the areas this line suits. For scenarios needing a human face and complex hand movement, conventional filming or a hybrid approach is the choice.

  2. 02

    The Production Flow and the Team's Role

    The process is run by a production director and an AI operator; in place of a camera crew, a technical team defines the scene, the movement and the lighting scenario. Every scene is checked twice — once before the render and once after.

  3. 03

    Quality Control and the Sign-Off Process

    Every generated scene is compared against the brand reference file and put through an internal check before it goes to the client for sign-off. Any inconsistency spotted before sign-off is corrected before it goes live.
