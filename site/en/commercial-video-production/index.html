From Script to Storyboard: The Pre-Production Stages

A commercial film begins with a script written by TasarımMania's copywriters in line with the brand brief.

The draft goes to the client for sign-off scene by scene; every revision is put on record in writing, which keeps the process clear. Once the script is approved it moves on to the storyboard stage. The storyboard is a checking layer that tests camera angles, scene transitions and timing against visual sketches before the shoot; when that stage is skipped, uncertainty on the shooting day and the risk of a reshoot both rise. Once the character design and the scene composition are settled, the team goes on location with a route map already in hand.

02

Animation or Live Action? The Format Decision

A live-action commercial rests on physical production elements: the cast, the location and the lighting setup. Once the storyboard is approved, the shooting plan and the location arrangements come into play.

An animated commercial, by contrast, rests on scene design, character animation and background illustration produced digitally. Both formats go through the same script-and-storyboard logic, but the production method differs at the root: brands aiming at abstract ideas or character-led storytelling usually go for animation, while stories that need a real location and a human subject favour the live-action format. In both formats the voiceover is recorded in a studio with professional voice artists and synchronised to the picture in the final mix. The claim the film makes is scrutinised too; Türkiye's commercial advertising rules hold the brand owner answerable for any exaggerated claim.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions: the commercial film shoot

Who writes the commercial film script, and how is client sign-off obtained?

The script is prepared by TasarımMania's copywriters in line with the brand brief. The draft is shared scene by scene, and every revision is tied to written sign-off so that no uncertainty is carried through to the shoot.

Does an animated commercial follow a different process from a live-action one?

Both formats start from the same script-and-storyboard foundation, but the production method diverges. The live-action format needs a physical location and a cast, while animation rests on character design and digital motion work.

Why is the storyboard stage never skipped?

The storyboard lets camera angles and scene transitions be tested visually before the shoot. When that layer is skipped, uncertainty on set and the risk of a reshoot both rise noticeably.

Is the commercial's voiceover recorded with a professional voice artist?

Yes — the voiceover is recorded in a studio by professional voice artists. The tone is directed to suit the brand's personality and synchronised with the picture in the final mix.

What determines the number of shooting days?

The shooting schedule takes shape around the number of scenes, the location changes, the cast and the equipment needed. Because those variables are settled at the storyboard stage, the shooting plan is ready before anyone goes on location.

Which channels can the delivered film be used on?

The delivered film is prepared in formats suited to different channels: television broadcast, digital ad networks, social media platforms and the corporate website. Channel-specific adaptations are handled within the video production service.
