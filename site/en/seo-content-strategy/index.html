Keyword Research and Search Intent Analysis

Before we build the content calendar we classify keywords not by volume but by search intent: informational, comparative and transactional queries are each handled separately.

Each intent group corresponds to a different content format and a different conversion goal. Using the topic cluster method we break the main headings down into subheadings, and tie each subheading to its own page or post. That clustering forms the backbone of the internal linking architecture: every new post links to the relevant pillar page and to the neighbouring cluster content, so the distribution of authority within the site proceeds to a plan. The variety of anchor text and the density of links are set out at this stage too, not left to chance.

02

How is the competitor gap identified, and what is the difference between SEO and GEO?

To identify the competitor content gap we compare the top-ranking pages in the sector by heading, subheading and the questions they cover; the sub-topics they don't touch and the questions they leave unanswered turn into new content opportunities.

This analysis brings out not only the missing words but the missing depth of explanation. The difference between SEO and GEO, meanwhile, changes the content plan at root: classic SEO focuses on keyword and link signals for ranking on the search results page, while GEO — visibility in AI search engines — calls for clearly defined, structured content that AI-assisted answer engines quote as a source. That is why in every SEO blog post we set out both the classic title-and-tag optimization and paragraphs that are self-contained and can be quoted directly. The ways content can appear in the search results are set out in Google's rich results gallery , type by type.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions: the SEO blog and content strategy

What's the difference between SEO blog content and an ordinary blog post?

An ordinary blog post is mostly written to inspire or to announce, while SEO blog content is built around a keyword, a search intent and an internal linking goal settled in advance. The heading structure, the subheadings and the meta description are all planned so the search engine classifies the page correctly.

What criteria is the content calendar planned on?

The calendar takes shape around the type of intent rather than search volume, around seasonal search trends, and around the gaps in the existing pages. No heading goes on the calendar until it is clear which cluster it will join and which pillar page it will support.

How does the difference between SEO and GEO change the content plan?

A classic SEO plan focuses on ranking signals, while a GEO plan calls for clear definitions and structured information that AI answer engines can quote directly. Headings that meet both goals are now included in the content calendar.

How is the competitor content gap identified?

The top-ranking competitor pages are examined by heading, subheading and the questions they answer; the topics they skip and the gaps in their depth are marked as new content opportunities.

Can our existing blog posts be updated, or are new ones written?

The priority is to update existing posts whose performance has slipped but whose potential is high; where updating isn't enough, a new SEO blog post is planned. The decision rests on the gap analysis of the existing page against its competitors.

Does content produced with AI tools carry any SEO risk?

The risk comes not from the tool itself but from the absence of editorial oversight. Content isn't published until it has been verified and made original by a human editor and settled into the internal linking architecture.
