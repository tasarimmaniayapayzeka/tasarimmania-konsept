Graphic Design Software Compared: Which Suits Whom?

Who will produce your brand's visuals, and with which tool? That is a matter businesses working with an agency need to settle on day one. Choosing between graphic design software really means building a production line. Photoshop, Illustrator, Figma and Canva do not do the same job. In this article we compare the tools at the level of features and use cases. Our aim is to make the choice that fits your team's budget and workload easier.

What Is the Fundamental Difference Between Design Applications?

Graphic design software solves four separate jobs: editing pixels, drawing vectors, designing interfaces and producing from templates. The difference lies not in the menus but in the logic of the output. Photoshop processes a photograph, Illustrator draws a form, Figma builds a screen, and Canva multiplies a ready-made skeleton within minutes. The right match comes out of the kind of work.

Taking the decision on the tool by looking at a feature list is misleading. We make the choice on the output. A catalogue page, a mobile screen and a social post ask for different production lines. In our graphic design services we build a separate chain for each job; which stage the file turns into which format is clear from the start. Graphic design software is only one link in that chain. Teams try to gather all the graphic work into just one of these applications. Yet a sound arrangement stands on two or three tools handing files to one another. If print weighs heavier, the vector tool comes forward; if the screen does, the interface tool does.

What does the raster–vector distinction mean?

Raster is the file format storing an image on a grid of pixels. Vector defines the shape with mathematical curves. That distinction affects your budget directly. A logo enlarged in raster breaks up at the edges. In vector, the same logo goes onto a sign as readily as onto a business card. A tool's competence depends on which file format it focuses on.

Why does the choice of licensing model matter?

The licensing model is the commercial framework setting a piece of software's monthly cost and the access within a team. Adobe's subscription runs per person, while Figma takes the number of seats as the basis and Canva a set of tiers starting from a free level. On a team of five, graphic design software forms a serious line in the annual budget. Read the clause on transferring seats before you sign.

Photoshop or Illustrator: Which Job Is Born in Which Tool?

Photoshop edits an existing image; Illustrator produces form from scratch. Photo retouching, banner composition and texture work are born in Photoshop. Logos, icon sets, packaging and signage are born in Illustrator. Teams that confuse the two come face to face with a resolution problem at the printing stage. Among graphic design software those two count as siblings, but their areas of duty are separate.

The mistake we see most often in the field is this: the logo is drawn in Photoshop. The file looks good on screen. When the printer asks for a three-metre poster, the job starts again. A brand with no vector source pays the cost of redrawing on every new channel. That is the most expensive mismatch among graphic design software.

The photography and retouching scenario

For e-commerce teams shooting product photographs, Photoshop is still the realistic option. Layer masks, smart objects and batch actions apply the same setting to hundreds of files in one go. You prepare the recording once and run it again on later shoots. Affinity Photo is a close alternative; verify the terms on the maker's current page.

The logo and brand identity scenario

Brand identity work asks for sharpness independent of scale. That is why Illustrator continues to be the agency standard. Its Pathfinder tools, grid system and typographic control are a step ahead of its rivals. Colour is a heading of its own at the printer; converting the source file to CMYK and defining the corporate colour as a spot colour secures the delivery. CorelDRAW, meanwhile, remains strong in signage and textile workshops.

The delivery file checklist

When you take delivery of a logo from your agency, ask for three files: the editable vector source, a PDF for print and an SVG for the web. Without those three in hand, your brand asset stays locked inside the tool.

How Does Figma Change Teamwork?

Figma takes design out of the exchange of files and into a shared room in the browser. The designer, the developer and the marketing lead look at the same screen at the same time. Comments accumulate on top of the file and version confusion disappears. On web and mobile interface projects, that arrangement alone can remove a round of approval.

When you renew a site or an app, the most expensive delay comes in the rounds of approval. A component library shortens that round. You change a button's colour centrally and a hundred screens update at once. Comments, approvals and revisions accumulate on the same link; email traffic falls. Developer mode closes the distance between design and code; because the developer reads the dimension, the colour and the spacing on the same screen, they ask the designer fewer questions. On our app interface projects and our corporate website work we run the approval flow with that structure. As a team grows, the graphic design software offering shared space comes forward.

Where is the limit of working in a browser?

Figma's strength gathers in screen design; on the print side it doesn't offer the same ease. It doesn't meet printing requirements such as CMYK colour management and bleed. It struggles with high-resolution photo editing too. For teams outsourcing their printing, that limit isn't a problem. Companies producing their own catalogue should set aside a separate vector tool: finish the screen in Figma and prepare the print file there.

Where Do Template Tools Like Canva Fall Short?

Canva is a cloud-based tool producing visuals quickly from ready-made templates. On social media posts, presentations and simple posters it does the job more than adequately. When an original brand language, complex print work or vector precision is needed, it hits a wall. Knowing the limit is far more useful than disparaging the tool. Set the expectation correctly and the result won't surprise you.

The number of businesses selling online is rising fast. According to ETBİS data — ETBİS is Türkiye's central e-commerce information system — the number of businesses trading online has reached 634,611. At that density, every brand puts out dozens of visuals a week. Template tools were born to carry that volume. But a template is a shared resource your competitor can reach too; you would struggle to tell two posts built on the same skeleton apart without the logos. Among the applications, template tools win on speed in graphic production and fall behind on distinction. Distinction for a brand comes only with original design.

The advantage of speed in producing social content

On a team putting out fifteen posts a week, the logic of templates works. Upload your corporate identity once and let the colours and typefaces come through automatically. On the social media management side we take that route for routine posts. The campaign visual is still drawn by a designer, because a template doesn't carry the brand voice. Separating routine from campaign protects both the speed and the brand's consistency.

Choosing Tools and Balancing Cost for SME Teams

The choice becomes clear where team size and volume of production intersect. In a one-person marketing unit, two applications are enough. In a structure of five, a shared library becomes essential. If the annual licence you pay for graphic design software stays below the outsourcing invoice, producing in-house makes sense. Otherwise, going with an agency comes cheaper.

When you discuss the budget, don't look at the subscription fee alone. The time it takes to learn is a cost item too. Introducing a tool to the team and migrating the old files takes time; that item doesn't show up in the licence fee but it does strain the schedule. On screen work Figma comes forward, on print work Illustrator does.

Matching by team size

On a one-person team, Canva and a single vector tool are enough. In a structure of three, Figma brings a shared space. On teams of more than five, the Adobe ecosystem starts to make sense as a full package. Before you buy, finish a real job on the trial version; take the decision from your own list.

Choosing a tool in five steps

  1. Sort the last three months' visuals by type.
  2. If much of the work goes to print, put the vector tool at the top of the list.
  3. Count how many people touch the same file.
  4. Compare the monthly licence cost against the outsourcing invoice.
  5. Try the tool you choose on a single campaign first, then roll it out.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions: graphic design software

Which software should a team just starting out begin with?

Look at your weekly production list first; the tool's reputation comes second. If most of your weekly work is social posts and presentations, start with Canva; the learning curve is gentle and the team gets used to it quickly. If you process product photographs, Photoshop becomes unavoidable. If you produce logos, packaging or signage, Illustrator is essential. If you draw web or mobile interfaces, Figma is the first tool to set up. Start with one application and add the second when the need arises. Teams trying to learn three tools at once usually go deep in none of them.

Why does drawing a logo in Photoshop cause trouble?

Photoshop stores the output as pixels. Enlarge the logo and the edges break up. On large-scale work such as signage, vehicle livery or a trade fair stand, the file becomes unusable. Choose a vector-based tool for a logo.

Is Figma's free version enough for a small business?

Small teams working on a single project with few files get by on the free tier for a long time. The limit starts with the number of editor seats and with the advanced library features. When you want to build a shared design system, you have to move to a paid plan.

Can we use visuals produced in Canva commercially?

Yes, but the licence status of every element you use applies separately. Some images on the free tier are restricted for commercial use. Take care not to produce your brand logo in that tool.

Is there a free alternative to Illustrator?

Inkscape is an open-source vector tool and handles basic logo work. On press compatibility, colour profiles and complex effects it makes its limits felt quickly. For small-budget beginnings it offers a reasonable first step.

Do the AI features in design tools genuinely help?

On work such as removing backgrounds, taking objects out and enlarging images, they shorten the preparation time. On producing an original composition in keeping with a brand's language, human judgement is still needed. We use those features at the preparation stage and leave the final decision to the designer.

How many pieces of software should a team producing both print and digital use?

Two tools are enough in most cases: let one take on the vector and print side and the other the screen design. If your volume of photography is high, add a raster editor as a third. More than that raises the licence cost without speeding production up.

When working with an agency, who should keep the source files?

The source files belong to the brand. Write it into the contract explicitly and ask for the editable versions on delivery.
