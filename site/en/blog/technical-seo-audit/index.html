What Does a Technical SEO Audit Uncover on Your Site?

Your site may not be showing up where it deserves to in the search results. The reason is usually not the content but technical problems that have never been measured. A technical SEO audit measures crawling, indexing, speed and Core Web Vitals performance with concrete metrics. In this piece we set out step by step which finding the audit measures against which threshold. Our aim is to offer measurement, not guesswork. A technical claim not backed by a figure can't be a sound basis for your investment decision. We take the measurement logic, the thresholds and the business impact of the findings through an SME's eyes.

What Is a Technical SEO Audit and What Does It Measure?

A technical SEO audit identifies, with metrics, the obstacles a search engine runs into on your site. At the end of the review you have clear numbers in hand. How many pages did the bot crawl, how many entered the index, which page was left slow? The report answers those questions one by one with a figure. That's how you point your budget at the most critical problem. You decide on measured data rather than guesswork.

The technical side of the audit photographs your site's infrastructure, not its visible surface. In the review we take on the robots.txt file, the XML sitemap and the redirect chains. We measure the canonical tags and the server response times with the same care. We tie every finding to a number: the count of broken links, the length of the chains, the number of pages left out of the index.

This measurement-led approach forms the basis of our technical SEO service . When the audit is done you have a technical priority list in hand. The list shows which problem is blocking how much traffic. For the strategy as a whole, you can look at our SEO services page .

The Audit's Four Areas of Measurement

A full technical SEO audit measures four areas: crawlability, indexability, site speed and Core Web Vitals. Crawlability quantifies bots' access to pages, indexability their acceptance into the index. Speed measures loading time, and Core Web Vitals the real user experience. Read together, the four produce the site's technical health report.

Which Problems Does the Crawling and Indexing Audit Uncover?

The crawling and indexing audit uncovers the pages bots can't reach and the content that doesn't enter the index. Redirect chains and duplicate pages also become visible at this stage. We express the findings as numbers. How many pages met an obstacle, how many addresses redirect in a chain, how much content was left out of the index? Only with those numbers do you read the size of the problem correctly.

At the crawl stage, the search engine bot discovers your pages by following links. The audit lists the obstacles in the way of that discovery one by one. The examples are familiar: sections accidentally closed off by robots.txt, and long redirect chains. Orphan pages that receive no internal links at all, and broken links returning 404, go on the list too. On the indexing side we examine whether the page the bot crawled is accepted into the index. A technical SEO audit scores those two stages separately. Whether the problem sits in the crawl layer or the index layer separates out clearly.

How Is Crawl Budget Measured?

Crawl budget is the amount of resource a search engine allocates to moving around your site in a given period. In the audit we compare the server log records with the crawl statistics. That's how we see whether the bot is spending its time on error pages or on important content. Wasted budget can point to serious loss of visibility even on medium-sized sites.

What Do the Gaps in Index Coverage Tell You?

The index coverage measurement compares the number of pages submitted with the number of pages that entered the index. The difference between them is one of the audit's most revealing findings. We separate that difference by cause: duplicate content, weak page quality or a server error. That separation also decides which team the fix work runs with.

How Are the Core Web Vitals Thresholds Read in an Audit?

Core Web Vitals is three metrics measuring loading, interaction and visual stability. The metrics are called LCP, INP and CLS. In the audit we classify every page group against those three thresholds. We identify the templates that exceed them. We order the fix priority by the volume of traffic affected. The result is a page-level improvement map.

The definition of each metric and the healthy threshold Google publishes are clear:

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint): the time the largest content element on the page takes to appear on screen. Under 2.5 seconds is considered good.
  • INP (Interaction to Next Paint): the time the screen takes to respond to a user interaction. We aim for under 200 milliseconds.
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift): the amount elements shift during loading. Under 0.1 is considered stable.

A technical SEO audit applies these thresholds to template groups rather than to individual pages. A problem in one product template affects hundreds of pages using that template at once.

Which User Experience Do the Metrics Represent?

LCP represents how quickly the page appears. INP shows how fast it responds to a tap, and CLS whether the content stays where it is. Your visitor lives those three feelings within seconds. The metrics only turn that experience into a number.

The Difference Between Field Data and Lab Data

Field data is measurement gathered from real visitors' devices. Lab data we produce in a controlled test environment. In the audit we assess the two together. Field data proves the problem exists; lab measurement helps find the root cause. Looking at only one produces an incomplete diagnosis.

How Do Site Speed Findings Affect Business Results?

Site speed findings show the root cause of slowness through measurement. The report measures the server response, the image sizes and the script load separately. A page that opens late raises the likelihood of the visitor leaving the site. The same delay also lowers the efficiency of your ad spend. That's why speed data is a commercial indicator in its own right.

The speed, or performance, audit measures three layers: the server's first response, the downloading of the resources and the browser's rendering of the page. Separating the layers turns a vague complaint into a fixable finding. The sentence “the site is slow” becomes the finding “the server is sending the first byte late”. The speed layer of a technical SEO audit shows exactly where the loss occurs.

According to the report announced by Türkiye's Ministry of Trade , e-commerce volume passed ₺3 trillion in 2024. The same announcement puts that volume's share of gross domestic product at 6.5%. TurkStat's latest research — TurkStat is Türkiye's statistical institute — also shows internet use spreading further every year. With the market growing at that scale, a site that opens late sends its customer to a competitor.

The Three Findings That Stand Out in a Speed Audit

Three findings stand out most often in audits: large uncompressed images, static files served without caching, and scripts that block the main thread. All three can be measured, and fixed without touching the content. Image compression alone can visibly shorten the loading time. The report estimates the expected gain by page group.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions: the technical SEO audit

How long does a technical SEO audit take?

The time varies with the size of the site. On an SME site of a few hundred pages, a full audit usually finishes in one to two weeks. On large e-commerce sites, the crawling and data collection can take longer.

Does a site that exceeds the Core Web Vitals thresholds lose rankings?

Core Web Vitals is one of Google's page experience signals. It doesn't decide rankings on its own. It can still make a difference when content of similar quality is competing. The indirect effect matters more. On pages that exceed the thresholds, visitors give up more often. That weakens the engagement signals and the conversion. That's why we also report a threshold breach as an indicator of lost users. We order the priority by the traffic and the commercial value of the affected page group.

What is LCP?

LCP measures how long the largest element the visitor sees takes to appear on screen. On most pages that element is the cover image or the heading block. Google recommends keeping the time under 2.5 seconds.

Do we need to give you access to our site for the preliminary audit?

No; we do the preliminary audit with externally accessible data. Admin panel or server access only comes up at the full audit stage. And we only take that step with your approval.

How often should the technical audit be repeated?

Always renew the audit after a major infrastructure or theme change. In regular maintenance, the common practice is a check every six months. On sites that add pages often, a quarterly check is safer.

Is a technical audit necessary for a small website?

Yes. Even with few pages, a single mistake can prove expensive. One wrong line in robots.txt can leave the whole site out of the index. On small sites the audit takes less time and costs less.

What does the audit report contain?

The report lists every finding under the crawling, indexing, speed and Core Web Vitals headings. Beside each finding sit the size of its effect and its fix priority. Every row is accompanied by a measured value and a target threshold.

Is INP different from the old FID metric?

Yes; FID measured only the first interaction while INP assesses every interaction, and FID is no longer in use.
