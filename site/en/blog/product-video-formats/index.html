E-Commerce Product Video Formats and Sizes

Assuming you can upload the same video everywhere is the most expensive assumption there is. A frame that sits perfectly on one channel leaves the product outside the frame on another. In this article we open up ratio, duration and file sizes channel by channel. The aim is to draw up the delivery list before the shoot; every crop made afterwards means another round of editing.

What Do You Choose a Product Video Format By?

Three things settle it: where you'll publish, how the viewer watches, and the shape of the product. The product video format emerges at the intersection of those three; it isn't a preference on its own. A tall product sits comfortably in a vertical frame; a wide table asks for landscape. And the place of publication dictates both the ratio and the duration together.

The order of decisions matters. Write the channel list first, then set the ratios, and build the shooting plan last. Go the other way round and you'll be forced to crop the footage you already have.

The four headings below appear on every delivery list.

  1. Aspect ratio — 16:9, 1:1 and 9:16.
  2. Duration — seconds or minutes, depending on the channel.
  3. File size — the upload limit and the streaming quality.
  4. Subtitles — burnt in, or a separate file.

Write the list into a table and share it with the team. Delivery requests passed on verbally get lost; a written list stands in front of everyone on the day of the shoot.

Is One Version Enough?

A square ratio offers a middle way and is accepted in most places. But on vertical channels it leaves most of the screen empty. The format decision is set by what your product page prioritises; looking average everywhere can mean looking good nowhere.

Which Aspect Ratio Works Where?

Three ratios cover the whole job. Landscape 16:9 works for the product page and the web, square 1:1 for feed content, vertical 9:16 for full-screen channels. When you choose the product video format, decide which of them is the main channel. Whichever it is, we build the shoot around it and derive the rest from there.

The vertical frame is the ratio most often misunderstood. Cropping a landscape frame to vertical means throwing away the sides; if the product isn't centred, it falls outside the frame.

That is why we leave a safe area during the shoot. Placing the product in the centre and keeping space at the edges makes it possible to get three ratios out of a single shoot. We took up the effect of the shooting location on that decision in the studio or location article.

The safe area rule is simple. Leave comfortable space at the edges of the frame; both the vertical crop and the buttons the channel interfaces overlay will eat into that space.

Can One Shoot Yield Three Ratios?

It can, if it was planned that way. Shooting wide and cropping works; you just have to allow for the loss of sharpness. On an unplanned shoot, the same job ends with half the product cut off.

How Long Should the Video Be?

Keep it short on the product page, and shorter still in the feed. On the page the viewer is already interested and can stay a little longer; in the feed, the first few seconds decide everything. The product video format covers duration too, because the short version of the same story is edited separately. Shortening is not the same as speeding up.

Producing a short version is a job of re-editing, not of cutting. The six-second form of a thirty-second piece isn't the same shots played faster; you have to decide which single message survives.

Work on the first second separately as well. In the feed the viewer sees only that much before scrolling on; showing the product in the very first frame is the safest route.

What Does the Short Version Drop?

It drops the context and keeps the product. The build-up and the usage narrative from the long version find no room in the short one; what's left is a single promise. Choosing that promise is what shortening is really about.

How Do You Balance File Size Against Quality?

Every channel recompresses the file, so the file you upload has to be at source quality. Compress it too hard yourself and, once the platform compresses it again, the product detail disappears. When you write the product video format down, add the sharpness and the frame rate too. A file that exceeds the upload limit gets mangled by the platform's own rules.

Keep the source file archived. If a new channel appears a year later and you have no high-quality source in hand, you'll be forced to produce a new ratio from a narrow version; the result gets markedly worse.

Colour and brightness vary from channel to channel as well. A tone that looks right on the product page can look flat inside an app with a dark interface; before delivery, it needs checking by eye on every channel.

File naming is part of the delivery too. Write the channel, the ratio and the duration into the name and nobody picks the wrong file on upload day. On a catalogue of thirty products, that habit saves hours.

Does Frame Rate Matter?

On most product videos it makes no difference. If there is fast movement or slow motion, you need to shoot at a high frame rate; otherwise the standard rate gives you a smaller file.

What Requirements Do the Marketplaces Look For?

Every selling platform has rules of its own, and they change over time. The common tendency is this: a clean background, the product clearly visible, the brand logo not brought forward, and a short duration. When you choose the product video format, read the current requirements from the platform's own documentation. Sizes picked up second-hand are usually out of date.

Read the rules while you're building the shooting plan, not on upload day. A requirement you learn about afterwards can render the footage you have useless.

We take up the relationship with the overall layout of the product page in our e-commerce service section. The rules for still images are a separate subject; we set those out in the product images article.

Allow for the rules changing, too. A list you drew up a year ago may be incomplete today; reviewing it once a year is enough. The easiest way to catch a change is to follow the announcements in the seller dashboard. Marketplace rules bind you, and so does the legislation; Türkiye's commercial advertising rules apply to the promise made in the video as well.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions: product video formats

How many separate versions should I prepare for the product video format?

It depends on how many channels you'll publish on, but three versions are a sufficient starting point for most businesses. A landscape or square version for the product page, a vertical version for full-screen channels, and a short version for the feed. With those three in hand, when a new channel appears you can usually adapt one of them and move on. Measuring which channel genuinely brings traffic before you grow the list protects you from producing for nothing. Multiplying versions without measuring only crowds the archive.

Does vertical video work everywhere?

No; on product pages, landscape or square usually sits better.

Can I crop the video to make it vertical?

You can if the shoot was planned with a wide frame. If it wasn't, part of the product falls outside the frame and the result is unusable.

Which resolution should I take delivery in?

Ask for the source file at the highest quality possible and keep it archived. Channel versions can always be derived from it; the reverse isn't possible.

Should the subtitles be burnt in?

For channels watched with the sound off, burnt-in subtitles are the safe choice. Subtitles delivered as a separate file, on the other hand, give you flexibility for multilingual publishing.

Can the music stay the same on every channel?

It can if your licence covers those channels. Publishing on a channel outside the scope creates an infringement.

Where do I find the marketplace requirements?

From the platform's own seller documentation. Because those rules are updated regularly, sizes read from third-party sources are usually out of date.

Should I autoplay the video on the product page?

Muted autoplay is both common and effective.
