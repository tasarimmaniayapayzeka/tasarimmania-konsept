The difference

“I had a website built, but…” — the difference between an off-the-shelf theme and a bespoke design

Most sites aren't broken; your content has simply been dropped into a template designed for somebody else's business. We don't install templates. And we don't describe the difference — we measure it: we put your page weight side by side with ours.

A site built on a template

Off-the-shelf theme + content filler

  • The code for the 40 features you don't use is downloaded by every visitor too; load time on a phone gets longer.
  • Because measurement is added later, nobody knows which page prompted the call.
  • A plugin is hunted down for every small change; every plugin is a new security surface.
  • When the theme updates, the design breaks and it's back to the agency again.
The system we build

A front end written for your business

  • The page carries only that page's code; the weight is low from the start.
  • The measurement schema is set up on day one; you know which form and which campaign did it.
  • You update the content from the panel yourself.
  • The source files and the accounts are yours.
FAQs

Frequently asked questions: corporate website timelines, costs and life after delivery

The questions that come up most, with short answers. Where the detail sits in another section, there's a link at the end of the answer.

How long does a corporate site take, and what actually stretches the schedule?

A corporate site is delivered in 15–30 working days. What really stretches the schedule isn't the code, it's the content: when the copy, photographs and logo files are late, every step after that slips.

We'and set out which job happens on which days in the table in the timeline section .

What happens if the project runs late?

When there's a delay we tell you and give you the new date in writing — we don't let it quietly drift. Because we break the schedule into stages, it stays visible which stage the delay came from and whose end it came from.

We'and listed the cause of delays and the remedies point by point in the “If things go wrong” section .

How is my number of revisions decided?

The number of revision rounds and what each round covers is itemised in the quote; you see it before you sign. There's also 30 days of correction support after launch, and any faults that come up in that time are put right free of charge.

Who prepares the copy and the photographs on the site?

The copy and the photographs come from you; neither is included in the price. If you need them, we plan copywriting and photography as separate line items.

Is an off-the-shelf theme or a bespoke design right for me?

An off-the-shelf theme is the right call for a business on a limited budget whose only expectation of the site is to be visible online. A bespoke design makes sense for businesses that expect measurable calls, enquiries and sales from the site.

We'and put a comparison table of the two approaches in the “the difference between an off-the-shelf theme and a bespoke design” section .

Should I have WordPress built, or bespoke software?

For most businesses publishing content and selling products, WordPress and WooCommerce are enough. Bespoke software is needed for the workflows an off-the-shelf system doesn't cover: dealer panels, booking, permissions management, processes that connect to an ERP.

Should I work with an agency, or is a freelancer enough?

For a one-person job a freelancer is cheaper and usually enough. An agency makes sense when design, software, advertising and content are running at the same time, and when the work has to still be standing in two years.

The difference isn't talent — it's continuity and scope. When one person falls ill or moves on, the project stops; in a team of eight the work carries on and your point of contact doesn't change. Who holds the source files, the panel credentials and the documentation is also put in writing. On the other hand, for a small one-off job an agency structure just adds cost.

Have you worked in our sector before?

Most likely yes: as of 2026 we'and completed 165 projects for 120 brands over 14 years, so the odds are good that we'and worked on something close. If we haven't worked in your sector, we say so in the first meeting.

We'and set out what changes from sector to sector in the “What changes by sector isn't the design” section .

What changes by sector isn't the design

What separates a hospital site from a hotel site isn't the colour palette — it's the moment the page has to rescue.

What a hospital or clinic website needs

The Ministry of Health's promotion regulations restrict prices, patient experience and before-and-after images in Turkish-language publishing; we build the content architecture through that filter.

What a hotel website should be: the road to the booking engine

The site's job isn't to close the sale — it's to carry the visitor to the booking engine already decided. A slow-loading gallery loses bookings.

On an e-commerce site it's the product page that earns, not the homepage

Most visitors land straight on a product page from a search result or an ad; we put the weight of the budget on the product page template, on-site search, and the variant and stock display.

B2B and industrial websites: catalogues, product codes, technical documents

Few visitors, high value per enquiry; what does the work is the technical documentation, product code search and a downloadable catalogue.

Nursery and school websites: built for the parent looking at a phone

The decision-maker looks on a phone, in the evening: location, photographs and one-tap calling.

Loading in 2.5 seconds on a phone: what the LCP, INP and CLS thresholds mean

Google's thresholds for “good” are clear: LCP under 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds, CLS under 0.1. The measurement is taken on mobile rather than desktop, at the 75th percentile of real user data — meaning three quarters of your visitors have to come in under that value.

At delivery we measure these three values before and after, and give them to you in writing.

The thing you mustn't lose when migrating an existing site: the URLs

A sentence we hear often: “we had a new site built and our traffic dropped”.

The cause isn't the design: the old URLs the search engine knows have changed, and if no equivalent is defined, visitors land on an error page.

The order we follow when migrating:

  1. We list every URL on the old site — pages, images and files included.
  2. We find which URLs were getting traffic and inbound links, from the measurement data.
  3. We write a new equivalent for every old URL; anything without one goes to the nearest meaningful page, not the homepage.
  4. The 301 redirects go live at the same moment as the site.
  5. The URL change is reported in Search Console and the sitemap is submitted.
  6. Error logs are watched for the first thirty days; any URL that slipped through is added the same week.

There's some fluctuation in the first two or three weeks while the search engine recrawls the new structure. Don't believe anyone who tells you it won't dip at all — what can be promised is that the cause won't be a migration mistake.

When you read a quote, look at the number of templates, not the number of pages

What drives the cost isn't the number of pages, it's the number of different templates .

6 PAGES · 6 TEMPLATES Pages that each need a different layout Homepage, service, blog, product, contact — six separate builds. HIGH COST

Whose name are the accounts in?

Whose job is KVKK, cookies and accessibility

The split is clear: the technical implementation is ours, the content of the legal text is your adviser's.

Ours: the cookie consent layer, the explicit consent box on the form, setting up the privacy notice page, basic server-side security.

Yours: your privacy notice and, where applicable, your distance selling agreement — your legal adviser writes them, and we wire them to the right page and the right flow.

On accessibility the basics are done from the start: a keyboard-navigable menu, a heading structure a screen reader can follow, readable contrast, alternative text on images. A full conformance audit is a separate piece of work — on public sector, healthcare and education projects we discuss its scope separately.
